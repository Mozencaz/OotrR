# Native combat and soul audit

Generated by `tools/audit_soul_combat.py` from the supported local game build.

## Scope and interpretation

Scanned **160 maps**, **1497 enemy instances**, **369 enemy AI groups** and **229 distinct modules**.

Every enemy is listed in `soul_combat_audit.json`, including optional enemies, excluded families, dream battles and the escape sequence. The table below lists native fight controllers, counter locks, scripted mechanics and bosses. Overlapping rows can describe the same battle; the record count is not a count of distinct architectural rooms.

Used death outputs, including generic actor controllers, identify scripted dependencies. A group containing enemies and a chest or shutter is treated conservatively as a fight. This may require more souls than the shortest playable route. Dungeon fights gate every entrance to that dungeon, rather than claiming an exact AP/remake room correspondence. Overworld chests use their native actor IDs, including merged child/adult copies. The Royal Tomb also gates its whole AP region, including the song.

Enemies excluded by `resolve_room_locks` remain present without a soul. Their fights still exist, but cannot create a missing-soul deadlock. A hidden shutter is never accepted as proof that its scripted collision or other barriers are gone.

A defeat signal with no reward/barrier dependency can be a lore flag or scene bookkeeping; these records are explicitly labelled, not presented as compulsory fights. This is a static data audit, not a playtest of every physical collision or undocumented engine behavior.

Uncovered dependencies in generated soul rules: **0**.

The JSON supplies stable actor and group IDs, enemy positions, requested outcome events, and matched AP reward checks for each controller.
